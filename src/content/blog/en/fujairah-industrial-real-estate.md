*DP World is accelerating its east coast expansion. For industrial real estate investors, the opportunity depends on what happens to the cargo after it arrives.*

More than one million containers, measured in twenty-foot equivalent units (TEUs), were moved by DP World from the UAE’s east coast ports to Jebel Ali and domestic markets between March and October 2026.

Imagine two containers arriving in Fujairah. One continues directly to Dubai. The other is unloaded, stored, processed and prepared for regional distribution.

Both contribute to port traffic. The second creates a very different opportunity for industrial real estate.

That distinction is becoming increasingly relevant as Fujairah’s port expansion gathers pace.

## An Earlier Opening Changes the Investment Timeline

On 28 July, I highlighted Fujairah’s industrial real estate potential following DP World’s announcement of an agreement in principle for a 50-year concession to develop two new terminals.

Four months later, the picture has become more interesting.

On 11 November 2026, DP World’s revised target for phased terminal openings was reported at 18–24 months, compared with the previous estimate of 24–30 months. The combined investment is estimated at AED 2.5 billion.

Once operational, the terminals are expected to increase DP World’s annual container-handling capacity across the UAE from 19.4 million to almost 22 million TEUs.

For businesses considering an east coast logistics operation, this brings questions about land, infrastructure and facility development closer to the present.

## Two Terminals, Different Real Estate Opportunities

The cargo profiles of the two planned terminals reveal where future industrial property demand could emerge.

Al Rugaylat Container and Multi-Purpose Terminal is designed for annual capacity of:

- Up to 2.5 million TEUs.
- 1.7 million tonnes of general cargo.
- 190,000 car equivalent units.

Dibba General Cargo Terminal will provide capacity for up to 3.6 million tonnes of general cargo annually.

These activities could support demand for distribution warehouses, vehicle storage, open industrial yards and specialised cargo-handling facilities.

The commercial potential of each property type will depend on which services businesses choose to perform locally and how much space they require beyond the terminals themselves.

## Is Fujairah Becoming an Alternative to Established Logistics Hubs?

In November 2026, Jebel Ali Free Zone (Jafza) reported warehouse occupancy of 99% and land occupancy of 94%.

Meanwhile, in May, Fujairah Terminals, part of AD Ports Group, signed three land lease agreements covering 130,000 sqm with Fujairah International Airport, Fujairah Free Zone Authority and Al Dahra Agriculture Trading.

These agreements are separate from DP World’s terminal development. They provide another indication that logistics infrastructure on the UAE’s east coast is expanding.

However, Fujairah’s integration with Jebel Ali also creates an interesting investment question.

The same transport network that makes Fujairah attractive as a maritime gateway allows cargo to move efficiently into established logistics hubs elsewhere in the UAE.

Where, then, will the economic activity associated with that cargo actually take place?

## Cargo Retention: The Real Estate Question

I use the term cargo retention to describe the proportion of cargo that generates local storage, processing, consolidation or distribution activity before continuing to its destination.

Some of the current east coast traffic reflects changes in regional shipping routes. As maritime conditions evolve, the distribution of cargo between UAE ports may change again.

For a warehouse investor, the decisive question is how much local demand will survive those changes.

A business that establishes a permanent distribution centre, processing facility or regional inventory base in Fujairah creates a different investment proposition from temporary transit activity.

This is where I would concentrate my attention.

## Three Tests Before Committing Capital

### 1. Cargo retention

Which cargo flows are likely to require permanent logistics services in Fujairah?

I would monitor sustained volumes, shipping patterns and the establishment of local processing, storage and distribution operations.

### 2. Occupier commitment

Which companies are prepared to establish facilities or enter into long-term leases?

Named occupiers, signed agreements and confirmed space requirements provide a stronger foundation for an investment decision. Individual sites also need to be assessed against land tenure, permitted activities, utility availability and transport access.

### 3. Location economics

Would operating from Fujairah make commercial sense compared with Jafza or another established UAE industrial hub?

The comparison should include annual occupancy costs per square metre and transport and handling costs per TEU.

Lower land or warehouse costs can be offset by additional transport expenses. The advantage depends on the entire operating model of the business.

## What Would Justify Investing in Fujairah Today?

The accelerated port development makes Fujairah a location worth investigating now.

For an industrial property investor, I would look for evidence of occupier demand before committing development capital. For a logistics operator, the decision begins with infrastructure readiness, property specifications and total operating costs.

There may be opportunities before the new terminals become operational, particularly where a specific business requirement can already be identified.
