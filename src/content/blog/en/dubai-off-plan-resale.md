*You've found a buyer, agreed on a price, and calculated your profit. But one clause in your off-plan purchase agreement could change everything.*

Imagine buying an off-plan apartment in Dubai. Construction is progressing, property prices have risen, and another investor is ready to pay more than you originally agreed to spend.

It looks like the perfect opportunity to take your profit and move your capital into the next investment.

Then comes an unexpected conversation with the developer.

Before you can obtain approval to resell the property, you may need to make another payment.

Suddenly, **to unlock the profit on your investment, you first have to put even more money into it.**

And that's where the real story of off-plan resale begins.

## 30%, 50% — or the Full Purchase Price?

There is no single payment threshold under Dubai law that automatically allows every off-plan property to be resold.

The conditions depend on the Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA), the specific development, and the developer's transfer procedures.

For example, one developer's published resale policy sets a general threshold of 30% of the purchase price. For certain projects, the requirement increases to 50% **plus the next advance instalment under the payment plan**.

That last part matters more than it might seem.

If the next scheduled instalment is substantial, the actual amount needed to qualify for resale could be significantly higher than the advertised 50% threshold.

Now consider a more restrictive contractual scenario: what if an SPA permits assignment only after the purchase price has been paid in full?

In that case, the strategy of reselling an apartment while only part of its price has been paid may no longer be viable.

A 100% payment requirement should not be assumed to apply to any particular project without checking its actual contract.

This is why I look beyond the headline percentage. What matters is **the total amount an investor must have paid before a resale can actually proceed**.

## When Rising Prices Don't Translate Into Available Cash

The requirement to make the next instalment can become particularly difficult when a large payment is tied to a construction milestone or handover.

You may have planned to sell before that point, precisely to avoid committing more capital.

But obtaining the developer's No Objection Certificate (NOC) may require you to satisfy additional payment obligations first.

Your successful exit now depends on two things: finding a buyer willing to pay the agreed price, and having enough liquidity to meet the conditions for transferring your rights.

Meanwhile, construction continues, instalments become due, and market conditions may change.

**A property's liquidity is not determined by buyer demand alone. It also depends on whether the transaction can be completed under the existing contractual terms.**

And there is another consideration: if the incoming buyer requires mortgage financing, eligibility for that specific off-plan resale must be confirmed in advance.

A profitable asking price means relatively little if the transaction cannot close when you need it to.

## The Three Questions I'd Ask Before Signing

If your investment strategy includes selling before handover, I would want clear answers to three questions:

1. **What percentage of the purchase price must be paid before resale is permitted?**
2. **Does the developer require the next instalment to be paid as well, even if it is not yet due?**
3. **How much cash must the investor actually commit before an NOC can be issued and the transfer completed?**

Then I would compare those obligations against the construction schedule, the intended holding period, and the capital available to the investor.

Two developments may offer similar locations, pricing, and potential appreciation. Yet one may allow you to release your capital at the right moment, while the other could require a substantial additional payment before you can exit.

That difference can reshape the investment decision.

## The Exit Is Part of the Investment

When evaluating an off-plan property, I pay attention to more than its projected future value.

I also want to understand how much flexibility the buyer retains after signing the SPA.

Sometimes an attractive entry price comes with restrictive exit conditions. Sometimes a different payment plan — or even a ready property — may better suit an investor's actual objectives.

**The time to understand your exit conditions is before you commit to the investment.**
