*A famous name can come with a substantial property price premium. But how much of that value remains once the marketing campaign is over?*

Imagine two residences in the same Dubai neighbourhood. Similar floor areas, attractive views, thoughtful architecture and impressive amenities. Yet one carries the name of an international brand and costs considerably more.

Which one would you choose?

At first, it might seem a matter of personal taste and budget. But when the purchase is also about preserving wealth, I find another question more interesting: **what exactly are you paying extra for, and will the next buyer see enough value to pay that premium too?**

That is where the real assessment begins.

## How Much Is a Famous Name Worth?

According to Morgan’s International Realty, branded residences in Dubai achieved an average price premium of 56% over the comparable non-branded properties used in its H1 2026 study.

CBRE reported a 64% average premium in its December 2025 research.

The studies cover different periods and property samples, but both reveal a substantial pricing gap associated with branded residential developments.

Those percentages, however, do not tell us the value of the brand itself. Location, architecture, views, unit size, services and other characteristics all influence the final price. A market-wide average cannot establish the fair premium for one particular apartment.

And that brings us to the first complication.

Buyers are paying for the promise of an exceptional living experience. Yet branded residences do not all deliver the same kind of experience.

## What Actually Comes with the Brand?

A hospitality operator, a fashion house, an automotive marque and a design studio can have very different roles in a residential development.

With a hospitality brand, the value may lie in everyday convenience: concierge services, staffing standards, property management and access to amenities. When these services are contractually defined and consistently delivered, they can make a meaningful difference to the owner’s life.

A design-led brand may focus more on architecture, materials, interiors and the character of the space.

Both approaches can be valuable. The question is how far the brand’s actual involvement extends.

An operator responsible for defined service standards is making a different commitment from a company licensing its name and design identity.

The distinction becomes particularly important after handover.

Who supervises the staff? Who maintains the amenities? How long does the branding or management agreement remain in force? What happens if the operator changes?

These questions rarely dominate the sales presentation. For someone planning to own the residence for years, they may be among the most important.

The legal classification and management model also deserve attention, as I explored in a separate article on [hotel apartments versus residential property in Dubai](https://www.ivandubai.xyz/en/blog/post-17/).

And even when the management is excellent, maintaining that standard comes at a cost.

## The Most Interesting Number Appears After the Purchase

A premium building requires ongoing investment in its upkeep.

Expansive common areas, expensive finishes, sophisticated engineering systems, landscaped gardens, swimming pools and professional staff all contribute to the experience. They also create recurring expenses.

In Dubai, owners pay service charges for the management and maintenance of jointly owned property. Approved charges can be checked through the Dubai Land Department and its Mollak system.

In a branded development, I would also establish which services are covered by those charges and which are billed separately.

Having a concierge, for example, does not necessarily mean that apartment cleaning, maintenance inside the residence or personal services are included.

I would be particularly careful with off-plan projects. A developer’s preliminary service-charge estimate may tell you relatively little about the actual operating costs of a completed building.

There is another question worth asking: **what will this property look and feel like in ten years?**

Luxury finishes need replacing. Technical systems require maintenance. Staff and service standards need consistent funding. The quality of management, provisions for major repairs and transparency of future costs are all part of the ownership proposition.

A brand can set expectations exceptionally high. Meeting them is an everyday responsibility.

But the real test comes later.

## What Happens When the Residence Reaches the Resale Market?

Morgan’s International Realty recorded 4,648 branded residence transactions in Dubai during the first half of 2026, worth AED 22.21 billion.

Properties under construction accounted for 82% of transactions by volume.

That last figure deserves attention.

At launch, a developer is selling a vision: architecture, an international name, a promised standard of service and a particular lifestyle. Much of the buying decision rests on expectations.

The secondary market operates differently.

The next buyer can inspect the completed building, observe the quality of maintenance, assess how the staff perform, understand the running costs and compare competing properties.

The name remains. Now the property has to live up to it.

This is why strong off-plan sales activity cannot, by itself, establish the future resale liquidity of a particular development.

I would want to see actual resale transactions, achieved prices, time on the market and the number of comparable properties available.

Future supply matters too. A concept that feels rare at launch may face several competitors by the time its first owners decide to sell.

For a buyer who expects to exit the investment eventually, that changes the calculation considerably.

## When Does the Premium Make Sense?

I would start by comparing properties with genuinely similar locations, floor areas, views, construction quality and completion status.

Then I would look at operating expenses, management, privacy, ownership conditions and the history of completed transactions.

Only after that would I examine the remaining difference in price.

A well-executed branded residence may offer very convincing advantages. Distinctive architecture, an exceptional location, reliable service and limited competing supply can all contribute to long-term desirability.

But buyers have different reasons for valuing those advantages.

For someone choosing a home, everyday comfort, atmosphere, privacy and thoughtful service may justify the expense regardless of the financial return.

For an investor, the entry price, net rental income, maintenance costs and resale prospects carry more weight.

For someone primarily concerned with preserving capital, I would pay particular attention to the property’s scarcity and the durability of demand beyond its original marketing campaign.

The same residence can be a wonderful place to live and a less convincing financial investment.

There is nothing inherently wrong with that. The important thing is knowing which outcome matters most before making the purchase.

## What Remains If You Remove the Brand Name?

There is a simple thought experiment I like to use.

Imagine the famous name disappearing from the building.

The architecture remains. So do the location, floor plan, views, staff, amenities, running costs and the feeling of being there.

**Would you still want to buy that residence at the same price?**

If the answer is yes, there may be enduring qualities worth investigating.

If most of the appeal disappears with the name, I would take a closer look at the alternatives.

This is how I prefer to approach luxury property selection in Dubai. When a client is considering a branded residence, I find it useful to put a strong non-branded alternative alongside it and examine the differences in registered sales, everyday living quality, ownership costs and resale prospects.

Sometimes that comparison confirms that the more expensive property is the right choice. Sometimes it reveals a better opportunity elsewhere.
