*A registered will is not only about inheritance. It coordinates access to money, property transfers, guardianship instructions, and cross-border estate documents.*

**Quick orientation.** Reported figures indicate that 49% of UAE expats live without a will, while 56% incorrectly assume their assets automatically transfer to their spouse. Without valid estate-planning documents, bank accounts and property transactions can be restricted during administration, and courts apply the relevant legal framework to inheritance and guardianship.

---

A marriage certificate does not replace a will, grant unrestricted access to a deceased spouse's accounts, or determine every aspect of child guardianship.

For UAE expat families, the practical risk is a gap between personal intentions and the formal process that begins after death. That gap can affect cash flow, real estate, minor children's shares, and the involvement of relatives in other countries.

The exact outcome depends on the deceased's religion, nationality, residency, asset location, family circumstances, existing documents, and the law and jurisdiction applied by the competent court.

---

## Default Asset Distribution Without a Will

Under the UAE civil inheritance framework applicable to qualifying non-Muslims, the surviving spouse generally receives 50% of the estate and the remaining 50% is divided equally among the children, regardless of gender.

If there are no children, other rules determine whether parents or siblings inherit.

Shares allocated to minor children legally belong to them. The surviving spouse may therefore be unable to use those funds freely for routine household expenses without the required legal authority or court process.

This is why estate planning must address both the intended ownership outcome and the family's immediate access to operating cash.

---

## Bank Accounts and UAE Property Can Be Restricted

Marriage does not provide immediate access to a late spouse's individual bank accounts. Banks may freeze relevant accounts while the estate is being administered and legal authority is established.

Real estate transactions can also stop. A sale, transfer, or refinancing involving property registered with the Dubai Land Department cannot proceed normally until the estate documentation and required legal procedures are completed.

In practice, the absence of a registered will can:

- Delay access to bank balances and investments
- Block pending property sales or transfers
- Create uncertainty over the deceased's business interests
- Require overseas documents, legalizations, and family participation
- Increase legal, translation, and administration costs

For property investors, succession planning is part of asset liquidity. An asset cannot be treated as fully liquid if no one has clear authority to manage or transfer it after the owner's death.

---

## Child Guardianship Requires Formal Instructions

Only 24% of expat parents reportedly hold formal guardianship documents.

Without legally valid instructions, the competent UAE court determines who will care for minor children if both parents die. The court applies the relevant law and the child's best interests; private conversations or undocumented arrangements with relatives do not create binding authority by themselves.

Parents should distinguish between two decisions:

- **Permanent guardianship:** Who should raise the children over the long term
- **Interim arrangements:** Who can care for the children in the UAE while permanent guardianship is being determined and implemented

A will and supporting guardianship documents should identify suitable people, alternatives, and any cross-border considerations affecting where the children will live.

---

## Cross-Border Wills Must Not Revoke Each Other

Families with assets in several countries face an additional risk: one will can unintentionally revoke another.

In a reported precedent, a will drafted in France revoked an earlier DIFC-registered will. The later document took priority, delaying asset distribution and creating financial pressure for the heirs.

Each document should therefore define its jurisdiction and asset scope clearly. UAE and overseas lawyers should review all wills together before a new document is signed.

The critical drafting question is not only whether a will is valid in isolation, but whether the complete set of documents works without contradiction.

---

## UAE Will Registration Options and Indicative Costs

Expats can use several registration authorities. Eligibility, document requirements, language, scope, fees, and probate procedures differ.

| Registration authority | Framework and language | Indicative registration cost |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| DIFC Courts Wills Service | English-language registry with its own probate framework | AED 10,000 for one Single Full Will; AED 15,000 for two Mirror Wills |
| Abu Dhabi Judicial Department (ADJD) | Onshore civil registration for non-citizens | AED 950 standard fee; AED 2,500 special registration |
| ADGM Courts | Notarization in English and Arabic; execution proceeds through ADJD because ADGM does not operate a probate court | AED 950 plus USD 155 per document |
| Dubai Courts | Onshore registration requiring Arabic documentation | Confirm current tariff directly with the court |

Legal drafting, translation, notarization, legalization, and professional advice are charged separately. Registry tariffs and procedures can change, so costs should be confirmed with the relevant authority before filing.

## A Practical UAE Estate-Planning Checklist

**1. Map the assets.** List UAE and overseas bank accounts, properties, company shares, investments, insurance policies, liabilities, and jointly held assets.

**2. Identify the applicable jurisdictions.** Record where each asset is located and which existing will or succession document covers it.

**3. Choose the appropriate UAE registry.** Compare DIFC Courts, ADJD, ADGM Courts, and Dubai Courts based on eligibility, language, asset scope, cost, and execution route.

**4. Document guardianship choices.** Name preferred permanent and interim guardians, plus alternatives, and confirm their willingness to act.

**5. Coordinate every will.** Use explicit jurisdictional limits and have advisers review the revocation clauses across all countries.

**6. Review after major changes.** Update the plan after marriage, divorce, childbirth, relocation, a property purchase, a business transaction, or a material change in residency.

---

## The Core Risk Is Loss of Control

Without a registered and coordinated will, the family does not simply inherit later. It enters a formal process that can restrict money, suspend property transactions, allocate assets under default rules, and leave guardianship decisions to the competent court.

The objective of estate planning is to make ownership, authority, jurisdiction, and guardianship clear before the family must rely on them.
